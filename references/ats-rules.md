# ATS formatting and wording rules

An ATS does two things: it **parses** the file into fields (name, contact, jobs, dates, education, skills), then recruiters **search and rank** candidates by keywords. A resume can fail at either step. Formatting rules protect parsing; wording rules help ranking.

## Formatting (parsing)

- **One column.** Multi-column layouts and text boxes get read out of order or dropped.
- **No tables, graphics, icons, photos, charts, skill bars.** Parsers skip or garble them. "Python ●●●●○" means nothing to an ATS.
- **Contact details in the body**, at the top — not in the Word header/footer, which many parsers ignore.
- **Standard section headings** the parser recognizes:
  - Summary (or Professional Summary)
  - Skills (or Technical Skills / Core Skills)
  - Experience (or Work Experience / Professional Experience)
  - Education
  - Certifications
  - Projects
  - Publications, Awards, Volunteer Experience, Languages (if relevant)
  Avoid creative headings like "My Journey" or "What I Bring".
- **Job entries in a consistent pattern:** Job Title, Company, Location, Dates. Keep the same order for every role.
- **Dates** as `Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY` or `MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY`, and `Present` for current roles. Be consistent. Years alone are acceptable but less precise.
- **Standard fonts** (HireKit uses Times New Roman; Calibri, Arial and Georgia are also safe), 10–12 pt body.
- **Simple bullets** (•, -). No custom symbols.
- **File type:** .docx is the safest. Text-based PDF is fine for most modern ATS. Never an image or scanned PDF.
- **No keyword tricks.** White text, tiny text or hidden keyword blocks get detected by modern systems and recruiters, and they get the candidate rejected.

## Wording (ranking)

- **Mirror the job's exact terms** for things the candidate really has. If the job says "Microsoft Excel" and the profile says "spreadsheets (Excel)", write "Microsoft Excel". If the job says "customer success" and they did "client account management", use the job's phrase if it's the same work.
- **Include both acronym and full form** once for important terms: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)", "Certified Public Accountant (CPA)".
- **Match the job title** in the summary where honest.
- **Keywords in context beat keyword lists.** Put the top skills in the Skills section *and* in experience bullets that show them used. Recruiters reading a ranked list want evidence.
- **Hard skills weigh most:** tools, languages, platforms, methods, certifications, regulations, domain terms. Soft skills ("communication", "leadership") count less — show them through outcomes rather than listing them.
- **Don't repeat a keyword unnaturally.** 2–3 natural mentions of a top term is plenty.

## Bullet formula

**Action verb + what you did + how / with what + result (number if the candidate gave one)**

- Weak: "Responsible for reports."
- Strong: "Automated weekly sales reporting in SQL and Power BI, cutting preparation time from 6 hours to 1."

Only use numbers the candidate provided. If a bullet would benefit from a metric you don't have, ask the candidate ("roughly how many customers / how much time saved?") or write it without one.

**Outcomes recruiters can read at a glance:**
- Every project, and every job role, shows at least one result. For projects, it's the last bullet.
- Best to weakest: a real number → a grade, award or ranking → real use or delivery → what it made possible.
- Write numbers as digits and bold them with what they measure: "Used by **300 students**", "cut report time **from 6 hours to 1**".
- Plain words, one line where possible. Never filler like "improved efficiency", and never an invented or rounded-up number.

Strong action verbs by type:
- Led / managed: Led, Directed, Coordinated, Supervised, Mentored
- Built / created: Built, Developed, Designed, Launched, Implemented
- Improved: Improved, Streamlined, Automated, Reduced, Increased, Optimized
- Analyzed: Analyzed, Evaluated, Forecasted, Modeled, Audited
- Communicated: Presented, Negotiated, Partnered, Trained, Advised

Start each bullet with a different verb where possible. Use past tense for past roles and present tense for the current role.

## Section order

- **Experienced candidates:** Summary → Skills → Experience → Education → Certifications → (Projects, etc.)
- **Students / new grads / career changers:** Summary → Skills → Education → Projects → Experience → Certifications. Put whatever is most relevant to the job first.

## Length and what to cut

- Let the content decide the length. One page is not a rule: most resumes are 1–2 pages, and academic CVs or long careers can be longer. Cut weak or unrelated items, not relevant real experience.
- Last 10–15 years of experience in detail. Older roles: a line each, or omit.
- 3–6 bullets for recent relevant roles, 1–3 for older or less relevant ones.
- Cut: "References available on request", objective statements, full street address (city/region is enough), photos, date of birth, marital status (unless local norms expect them — e.g. some countries — and the candidate asks).
