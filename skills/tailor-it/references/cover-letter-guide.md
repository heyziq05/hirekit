# Cover letter guide

The HireKit cover letter is a **formal letter**: sender address, date, recipient address, "Dear Sir/Madam,", a bold underlined subject, then **four numbered paragraphs**. This is the style expected for job and internship applications in Malaysia and many other countries. Use `assets/cover-letter-template.md` for the exact layout.

Many ATS store the cover letter and recruiters search it, so it should use the job's key terms. But its main reader is a person deciding whether to call the candidate, so it should answer: why this role, why this company, why you.

## The four paragraphs (about 300–450 words in total)

1. **Introduction and availability (3–5 sentences).** Name the exact role and company. Say who the candidate is (year and degree, or current role), give one or two standout facts (CGPA, Dean's List, years of experience), and state availability: internship dates, notice period, or willingness to relocate.
2. **Matching skills and proof (3–5 sentences).** The candidate's real skills that match the job, in the job's words, backed by one or two specific projects or achievements with results.
3. **Why this company, and what they'll give (4–6 sentences).** Something specific from the job post or company (its work, market, values). What the candidate hopes to learn, then what they'll contribute in return, tied to real experience.
4. **Close (2–3 sentences).** Resume attached, would appreciate being considered, ready to provide more information.

After the paragraphs: "Thank you for your consideration.", "Yours sincerely,", space to sign, then the signature block (bold name, degree or title, university or company, phone and email, LinkedIn).

## Rules

- **Same honesty rule as the resume.** Only real experience. A gap can be addressed honestly: "My deployment experience so far is Docker on a single server, and I'm keen to build on it with Kubernetes."
- **Statements about what the candidate *hasn't* done are claims too.** Missing from their notes only means they didn't mention it. Unless a gap is confirmed, use soft wording ("my experience so far is…") and mention at most one gap. Lead with strengths.
- **Don't invent company facts or addresses.** Use only what's in the job post or the user's info. If there's no company address, use just the department and company name.
- **Salutation:** "Dear Sir/Madam," unless a name is known ("Dear Ms. Aina,").
- **Subject line:** "Application for <exact job title>" (the builder renders it in capitals).
- **Don't repeat the resume** line by line. Tell the story behind one or two points.
- **Tone:** formal, confident and specific. Cut empty phrases like "I am a highly motivated team player". Show it with an example instead.
- **Length:** usually about one page, but it can run over if the content is relevant. Cut repetition, not real evidence.
