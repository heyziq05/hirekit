# Scoring rubric

Score each answer 1–5 overall, plus three sub-scores. Be calibrated. A 5 means the answer would clearly impress a real interviewer for this level, not just "no mistakes". Judge against the job's level: a fresh graduate isn't expected to have senior-level stories.

## Sub-scores

**Structure (1–5).** Is the answer easy to follow?
- 5: Clear shape (STAR for behavioural questions, a logical approach for technical ones), right length (about 1–2 minutes spoken, roughly 120–250 words typed).
- 3: Understandable but meandering, or missing a part (usually the result).
- 1: Rambling, no clear point, or one line.

**Specificity (1–5).** Is there real evidence?
- 5: A specific situation, the candidate's own actions ("I", not just "we"), concrete details and a result, ideally measurable.
- 3: Some specifics, but generic in places, or the result is vague ("it went well").
- 1: Hypothetical or generic ("I'm a hard worker", "I would…") when a real example was asked for.

**Relevance (1–5).** Does it answer the question and connect to this job?
- 5: Directly answers it and links to something the job needs.
- 3: Answers it, but the link to the role is left to the interviewer.
- 1: Answers a different question or dodges.

**Technical questions:** use **correctness** (is it right, at the level the job expects?) and **reasoning** (do they explain trade-offs, ask clarifying questions, check their own answer?) instead of specificity, where that fits better.

## STAR, briefly
- **Situation**: context in 1–2 sentences.
- **Task**: what they needed to achieve.
- **Action**: what *they* did, which should be most of the answer.
- **Result**: outcome, with a number if they have one, plus what they learned.

The most common weaknesses: too much Situation, "we" instead of "I", and no Result.

## Overall verdict (as the interviewer would decide)
- **Strong yes:** consistently specific, relevant answers; clearly meets must-haves; good questions at the end.
- **Yes:** solid overall with one or two weaker answers.
- **Lean no:** several vague answers, or a must-have not convincingly shown.
- **No:** mostly generic answers, red flags, or clear mismatch with must-haves.

Explain the verdict in one or two sentences, pointing to specific answers.

## Red flags to call out
- Criticising a former employer, manager or colleague.
- No example when asked for one, repeatedly.
- Claims that fall apart under a follow-up question, or that conflict with the resume.
- Salary handled badly: a number with no reasoning, or an aggressive or evasive answer.
- Answers far too long (over 3 minutes) or far too short.
- Little knowledge of the company or the role.
- No questions for the interviewer.
- For gaps: pretending to have the skill rather than addressing it honestly.

## Strong answers to gap questions
Coach this pattern instead of bluffing:
1. **Acknowledge** briefly: "I haven't used Kubernetes in production."
2. **Bridge** to related real experience: "I've containerised and deployed services with Docker."
3. **Plan**: "I'm working through [course they named], and I'd expect to get up to speed by [doing X] in my first months."
